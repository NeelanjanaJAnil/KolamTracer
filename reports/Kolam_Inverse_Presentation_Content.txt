[SLIDE CONTENT - BULLET POINTS]
* The Tradition of Kolam:
  - An ancient South Indian ritual art form drawn with rice flour at house thresholds around a grid of dots (pulli).
  - Passed down orally and kinesthetically across generations as cultural geometry.

* The Computational Nature of Kolams:
  - Not unconstrained sketches: Kolams represent continuous, non-intersecting Eulerian loops.
  - Planar graph embeddings with strict topological closures and dot encirclement.
  - Symmetries: Dihedral reflection groups (D4 and Klein-4 V4) and fractal self-similarity.

* The Inverse Problem:
  - Forward generation is straightforward: executing a grammar or turtle-graphics sequence.
  - Inverse reconstruction is difficult: given only noisy image pixels, infer the continuous mathematical equations (lines, arcs, Bézier curves) and the generative grammar.

SLIDE 02: PROBLEM STATEMENT & MOTIVATION
================================================================================
Slide Title: Problem Statement & Motivation
Subtitle: The Fundamental Failure of Autoregressive Neural Decoders in Geometric Inverse Graphics

[SLIDE CONTENT - BULLET POINTS]
* The Discrete-to-Continuous Semantic Gap:
  - Images exist as discrete, pixelated sensor grids with noise and anti-aliasing.
  - Target curves exist in continuous real coordinate space (R^2).

* The Catastrophic Drift of Autoregressive Models:
  - Standard deep learning sequence decoders (e.g., SketchRNN, Transformers) predict coordinates (x_t, y_t) step-by-step.
  - They suffer from compounding positional error: over long trajectories (700+ steps), small 1-pixel drifts compound exponentially.
  - Result: By step 200, loops fail to close, leaving open dangling curves and drawing spurious 'jump lines' across the canvas.

* The Topological Blindness of Standard Loss Functions:
  - Losses like Mean Squared Error (MSE), L1, and standard Chamfer distance only measure spatial proximity.
  - They are mathematically blind to graph invariants: vertex degree parity (d(v) = 0 mod 2), loop enclosures, and Betti cycle counts.

[SPEAKER NOTES / TALKING POINTS]
"Why can't we simply apply off-the-shelf deep learning to this problem? If you train a Transformer or an LSTM to output coordinate points sequentially, an error of just one pixel at step 30 snowballs by step 700. The loop fails to close, producing an invalid open curve. Furthermore, standard loss functions like MSE are topologically blind: they do not penalize a model for breaking loop connectivity or missing dot anchors."

SLIDE 05: SYSTEM WORKFLOW & ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================
Slide Title: System Workflow & Architecture
Subtitle: The Six-Stage Pipeline: From Pixels to Symbolic Geometry

[SLIDE CONTENT - THE SIX STAGES]
Stage 01: Raw Input Capture
  - Ingestion of calibrated pulli lattice scans or arbitrary user uploads.

Stage 02: Cleaned Mask
  - Otsu thresholding + Morphological Area Opening (<= 16 px).
  - Eliminates isolated pulli dots while preserving thin stroke loops.
  - Verified: Exactly 0.0 px stroke skeleton erosion across all test designs.

Stage 03: Medial Skeleton
  - Zhang-Suen morphological thinning algorithm produces a 1-pixel medial axis.

Stage 04: Graph Extraction & Trajectory
  - Junction detection via 8-connected neighborhood lookup:
      degree(p) = sum(|I(p_i) - I(p_{i+1})|) / 2
  - Straight-through crossing rule: at 4-way crossings, choose outgoing branch maximizing cosine similarity (max cos theta) to prevent false kinks.

Stage 05: Piecewise Equation Fitting
  - Orthogonal Distance Regression (ODR) fitting lines, circular arcs, and cubic Béziers.

Stage 06: Verification Overlay
  - Mathematical equations rasterized onto a canvas and compared to the original skeleton using Euclidean distance transforms.

[SPEAKER NOTES / TALKING POINTS]
"Here is our complete system architecture. Notice Stage 2: we use morphological opening to eliminate pulli dot anchors while rigorously confirming that exactly zero pixels of the stroke skeleton are eroded. In Stage 4, crossing junctions are traversed using the straight-through rule, maximizing cosine similarity between incoming and outgoing directions to prevent artificial kinks."

SLIDE 06: MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATIONS
================================================================================
Slide Title: Mathematical Foundations
Subtitle: Continuous Differential Primitives, Dihedral Groups, and Grammar Induction

[SLIDE CONTENT - MATHEMATICAL FORMULATIONS]
1. Closed-Form Primitive Equations:
  * Straight Line Segment:
      Parametric: r(t) = (1 - t)*p0 + t*p1,  t in [0, 1]
      Implicit:   (y1 - y0)*x - (x1 - x0)*y + (x1*y0 - x0*y1) = 0
                  Domain: valid for x in [min(x0,x1), max(x0,x1)], y in [min(y0,y1), max(y0,y1)]
  * Circular Arc:
      Parametric: r(t) = [h + r*cos(th0 + t*delta_th),  k + r*sin(th0 + t*delta_th)],  t in [0, 1]
      Implicit:   (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2
  * Cubic Bézier Curve:
      Parametric: B(t) = (1-t)^3*P0 + 3(1-t)^2*t*P1 + 3(1-t)*t^2*P2 + t^3*P3,  t in [0, 1]
      Fitted via Levenberg-Marquardt non-linear least squares minimizing perpendicular residuals.

2. Group Theory: Klein-4 (V4) Dihedral Group Orbit Reduction:
  * Symmetry group V4 = { e, sigma_x, sigma_y, R_180 } acting around center (cx, cy):
      sigma_x(x, y) = (2*cx - x, y)       [Horizontal reflection]
      sigma_y(x, y) = (x, 2*cy - y)       [Vertical reflection]
      R_180(x, y)   = (2*cx - x, 2*cy - y) [180-degree rotation]
  * Storing only the canonical seed quadrant Q1 regenerates the entire design, yielding an exact ~2.9x parameter reduction.

3. Formal Language: Re-Pair Grammar Induction:
  * Recursively replaces the most frequent digram pair (A, B) with a new non-terminal rule R_k.

"Every piece in our model is a rigorous mathematical object. Lines and arcs are provided with both parametric and algebraic implicit representations with bounded domains. For Béziers, we fit four control points via orthogonal distance regression. Using Klein-4 group theory, we mathematically prove that every dataset Kolam can be regenerated from its canonical seed quadrant, eliminating four-fold redundancy."

SLIDE 11: SCOPE, LIMITATIONS & CRITICAL LESSONS
================================================================================
Slide Title: Scope, Limitations & Critical Lessons
Subtitle: Honest Boundary Conditions and Failure Modes

[SLIDE CONTENT - LIMITATIONS & LESSONS]
1. Deterministic vs Neural Paradigms:
  - In unstructured vision tasks (e.g. object recognition), deep learning dominates.
  - In structured, graph-constrained geometric lattices, deterministic algorithms with mathematical priors achieve 99.94% accuracy vs 82.01% for CNNs.

2. Sensitivity of Planar Lattices to In-Plane Rotation:
  - The model is robust to Gaussian noise (sigma=0.05, 79.56% F1), blur (1px, 80.31% F1), and JPEG compression (80.30% F1).
  - However, it degrades under in-plane rotation (2-degree tilt drops F1 to 31.36%) because rotated lattice vertices drift away from orthogonal sampling points.

3. Real-World Mobile Photos Require Calibration:
  - The pipeline is validated on calibrated, orthogonal scans. Real-world mobile photos with perspective tilt or harsh shadow gradients require 4-point homography calibration before edge inference.

[SPEAKER NOTES / TALKING POINTS]
"A critical lesson of our research is that deep learning is not always the best tool for every vision problem. When mathematical constraints are absolute, a hybrid architecture—using computer vision for feature extraction and discrete mathematics for traversal and fitting—guarantees topological validity. We also honestly acknowledge that real-world mobile photos with camera tilt will require perspective homography correction."

Slide Title: Anticipated Faculty Q&A Defense Strategy
Subtitle: Prepared Strategic Responses for Reviewers

Question 1: "Why did your CNN only achieve 82% when modern vision models easily get 95%+?"
Strategic Defense:
"Standard vision benchmarks classify high-level semantic categories where global texture suffices. Here, edge classification requires sub-pixel coordinate alignment. When edges are separated by only 0.11 lattice units, small receptive field misalignments cause false positives. The deterministic detector achieved 99.94% because it projects directly along the known lattice geometry."

Question 2: "Why is the topology preservation box showing ~43% in the screenshots?"
Strategic Defense:
"The 43.7% Betti number preservation is an artifact of the raster comparison: the thresholded image mask contains pulli dots that create extra topological holes (beta_1), while our piecewise equations are fitted per branch and have sub-pixel gaps at junctions before node snap-rounding. We recognized this discrepancy and honestly marked it 'under correction' rather than presenting misleading claims."

Question 3: "Can your model handle a photo taken from an angle with a smartphone?"
Strategic Defense:
"Currently, the pipeline requires an orthogonal view. If taken at an angle, perspective foreshortening distorts regular lattice spacing. In future work, we plan to incorporate a 4-point homography estimation step using the pulli dots as vanishing point references to unwarp photos before feeding them to the equation engine."

Question 4: "Why did you choose Klein-4 symmetry over standard rotation?"
Strategic Defense:
"While some Kolams have 8-fold dihedral symmetry D4, all canonical pulli Kolams in this benchmark strictly obey the Klein-4 group V4 (horizontal and vertical reflections). We proved empirically that 100% of the true edge sets across all 600 designs are invariant under V4, providing an exact 2.89x to 2.94x parameter reduction without loss."
